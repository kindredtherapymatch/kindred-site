Why the match matters

The person in the chair is not a detail.

Therapy uses methods. What most reliably predicts whether it works for you is whether you and this person can actually work. Directories treat therapists like a filter. The research treats the relationship like part of the treatment.

The relationship tracks with better outcomes

Across 295 studies and more than 30,000 people, the quality of the working alliance — agreement on what you’re doing, and a real bond — is one of the most consistent predictors of how therapy goes. It holds in the room and on video, and it still holds after you account for how someone was doing when they started.

Fit is how people stay

When clients get a therapist and a style they actually prefer, they improve more and they are about a third less likely to leave early. Matching how a therapist works to how you work is not a nicety. It is one of the more reliable ways to improve the odds.

Who the therapist is matters

The therapist’s half of the relationship matters more than the client’s. Some therapists form a working alliance across a range of people, and those clients do better. Who you sit with is not interchangeable — and assignment that ignores that does worse.

The research this is based on
  • Flückiger, Del Re, Wampold & Horvath, 2018. Meta-analysis of the alliance in adult psychotherapy: 295 studies, r ≈ .28 with outcome — a medium effect, and one of the most replicated findings in the field. Same pattern in internet-based therapy. Summary
  • Flückiger et al., 2020. The alliance still predicts outcome after you adjust for intake characteristics and other process variables. Open access
  • Norcross & Wampold, 2019. Third APA interdivisional task force on evidence-based relationships: the relationship contributes to improvement as much as the particular treatment method.
  • Swift, Callahan & colleagues. Clients matched to their treatment, role, and therapist preferences have better outcomes and are about a third less likely to drop out.
  • Del Re, Flückiger, Horvath, Symonds & Wampold, 2012. Therapist variability in the alliance matters more than patient variability.
  • Constantino et al., 2021. Matching patients to therapists with a track record on their presenting concerns improved outcomes versus usual assignment. PCORI report

What Kindred does with that.
